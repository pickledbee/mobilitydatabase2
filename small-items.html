Small Items and Accessories

Everyday aids, accessories, recovery items and home safety products, with a buying tip for each.

Not medical advice. These are everyday aids. Ask your doctor or an occupational therapist what is safe for you, especially for transfer aids, bed rails, crutches and walking boots. For wheelchairs, scooters, lifts, ramps and hospital beds, see used equipment listings or our local businesses.

Local mobility stores focus on bigger equipment. Many of the small items below are not sold in a showroom, so people often buy them online. Each item has a short buying tip. The link shows Amazon search results so you can compare brands, prices and reviews yourself.

Daily living aids

Small tools that make dressing, eating and getting around the house easier.

Reacher grabber tool

Pick up items without bending or stretching.

Buying tip: Look for a lock on the jaws and a length of 26 to 32 inches. A light one is easier on weak hands.

Sock aid and dressing stick

Put on socks and clothes without bending.

Buying tip: Choose a sock aid with long, soft straps. A flexible plastic trough is easier to slide on than a rigid one.

Long-handled shoehorn

Buying tip: A 24 inch handle suits most people. Metal lasts longer than plastic.
