Aging in Place: Green Bay and Appleton

Free local help, home care and equipment contacts for Brown and Outagamie counties.

This guide is for families in Green Bay, Appleton and nearby towns who want to stay safe and independent at home. Start with the free county help, plan the changes, then call the businesses you need.

The ADRC is a free public service. They can explain what help and programs you may qualify for, and who to call. Elsewhere in Wisconsin, the statewide number 844-947-2372 (844-WIS-ADRC) connects you to your county's ADRC.

2. Plan the changes to the home

Walk through the house with the person who lives there. Common changes, from simplest to biggest:

  • Lighting and clutter. Bright lights, night lights on the way to the bathroom, and clear walkways.
  • Bathroom. Grab bars, a shower seat, a raised toilet seat, and a non-slip mat.
  • Stairs. A second handrail, or a stairlift. Try our stairlift cost estimator.
  • Entry. A ramp or a no-step entry, and doors at least 32 to 36 inches wide for a wheelchair. Try our ramp length calculator.
  • Bedroom. A bed at the right height, a bed rail, or an adjustable bed.
  • Mobility equipment. A walker, scooter, lift chair or wheelchair. See the Fit Finder and Price Guide, or browse used equipment near you.

An occupational therapist can visit or assess the home and recommend what will work. Ask the person's doctor or the ADRC for a referral.

4. Questions to ask any contractor or agency

  • Are you licensed and insured? Can I see proof?
  • Do you have the state credentials this kind of work needs? (You can check with the Wisconsin Department of Safety and Professional Services.)
  • Can I have the price, what it includes, and the schedule in writing?
  • For care agencies: how are caregivers screened and trained, and what happens if the caregiver is sick?
  • Can you give me two references from nearby customers?
  • What is the warranty, and who do I call if something goes wrong?
